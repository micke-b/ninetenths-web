User manual

One finger, no buttons

Everything is a tap or a swipe anywhere on the glass, so there is never a button to find while driving. Try each one on the live gauge.

TapNext page. The gauge starts on the page you left it on.
Swipe left or rightNext or previous page.
Double tapSettings: brightness and burn-in protection; swipe for the hot tire targets, then the units. Tap the − and + bars to change a value and DONE to leave. Settings close by themselves after 15 seconds.
Tap a warningA warning takes over the screen. A tap acknowledges it; it stays as a red tag at the bottom until the value recovers.
Start and stopThe gauge wakes with the car and switches off when the car goes to sleep. Tap during the start-up animation to skip it.

Set up by GR Yaris drivers

Our setup, not your homework

Most dashes hand you a layout editor and a list of limits to look up. We made those calls for the G16E and the GR-Four driveline, so the gauge is right the first time you plug it in.

Warnings

Red means look now

Red is used for nothing else. When a limit is passed the warning takes over the whole screen until you tap it, then stays as a tag at the bottom until the value recovers. Try one on the gauge.

OILOil over 130 °CClears under 125 °C.
WTRWater over 110 °CClears under 105 °C.
OIL POil pressure under the minimum for the rpm0.5 bar at 1000 rpm rising to 1.5 bar from 4000, engine running.

The display

Built around the screen

A gauge is only as good as what you can read in half a second at speed. So the screen came first: an AMOLED whose black pixels are switched off, our own renderer written for it, and three colors that only ever mean one thing.

GreenGo. In range, READY, on target.
OrangeLook soon. Near a limit, lean, knock, not warm yet.
RedLook now. Warnings and the shift light, nothing else.

Values change the moment a new reading arrives; nothing is animated in between. A reading older than a second dims; older than three it turns to dashes. The gauge never shows a stale number as if it were live.

Install

Plug in. Drive.

Plug it into the OBD port

Under the dash on the driver's side. One cable to the gauge; unplug it whenever you need the port for a scan tool.

Clip the pod to a vent

The round gauge sits in a vent pod at eye level, in reach of a thumb for paging through.

Start the car

The gauge wakes with the car, opens on the page you left it on and switches itself off when the car goes to sleep.
